What makes a practice partnership work

A peer partnership only feels safe if the norms are actually good ones — a group can easily recreate the same judgement people are trying to escape. Sort these into what tends to help versus what tends to hurt.

Part 2 · Try it

Draw a topic card

Unstructured conversation creates its own anxiety — the uncertainty of who speaks next, and about what. Topic cards remove that by handing you a starting point. Draw one, and use it in your next practice session: discuss it for two to five minutes before drawing another.

A familiar scene

A lunchtime conversation group

Three learners, once a week, very informal

She almost said no to joining. What if the others were better than her? At the first session, someone said "I am here since two years," laughed at herself, and moved on. When my student stumbled over "hiking" and described it as "walking in the mountains," the response was simply, "Ah, hiking! Where did you go?"

No judgement. Just people trying to communicate, all in the same position. Four weeks later, she was speaking more freely than she had in years. Nothing about her English had changed — only who she was practising with.
