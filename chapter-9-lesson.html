A familiar scene

Three days for a five-minute email

A simple request for time off

She wrote a short request, decided it sounded too brief, added detail, worried it sounded too personal, deleted it, rewrote the opening line three times. Two hours later, still unsent. By the time she finally sent it, three days had passed and the dates were already booked.

Next time, she gave herself a hard five-minute limit. Type, read once, fix one typo, send. Her manager replied within ten minutes: "No problem, approved, thanks for letting me know." Hours of agonising the first time; five minutes and an identical result the second.

Part 3 · Reference tool

Find the phrase, skip the agonising

Half of writing anxiety is staring at a blank line trying to invent wording from nothing. Pick the situation you're writing for, and borrow the phrase instead.
