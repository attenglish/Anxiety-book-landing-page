Chapter 2 · Foundations

Rewriting your
language learning story

The belief that you're "bad at English" was never a fact. It's a story someone helped you write — a teacher's correction, a classmate's laugh, an interpretation you made at fourteen and never revisited. This lesson helps you separate what actually happened from the meaning you attached to it, and gives you a thirty-second technique for responding to yourself with more kindness.

Step 1 · Mindfulness
"This is a moment of difficulty."

Acknowledge what you're feeling, without judging it.

A familiar scene

Where the story began

A colleague, now working internationally

He had a genuinely useful idea during a video call with the London office, and stayed silent because he was worried about his grammar. Someone else raised a similar point minutes later and was praised for it. Afterwards he felt furious with himself: "Why can't I just speak? I'm not stupid. I know English."

He wasn't the problem. The story he was carrying about what mistakes mean was the problem — and stories like that can be rewritten.

Part 1

Facts versus interpretations

A fact is what actually occurred. An interpretation is the meaning you assigned to it. Facts are neutral; interpretations carry emotional weight — and unlike facts, interpretations are negotiable. Read each line below and decide which it is.
